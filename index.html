~/shared-calendar v1, Android

Shared Calendar

A calendar for two, built for rotating shift work.

Ordinary calendars handle irregular rotas badly. Shared Calendar is designed around entering shifts quickly: pick a shift from a palette, tap the days, and a week takes about five interactions instead of thirty-five. Each partner sees the other's shifts labelled and coloured, without being able to read or edit their jobs.

  • Household of two, with invite codes and QR joining
  • Shifts belong to jobs, jobs belong to a person
  • Password reset by emailed code, JWT sessions with refresh
  • One web build, wrapped by Capacitor for Android

Shared Calendar for iOS

The iPhone build of the same calendar.

~/game-browser v1.0

Game Browser

Every installed game, one visual grid.

A local desktop app that scans your installed games across Steam, Epic, Xbox, Battle.net, Riot Games and Ubisoft Connect and shows them together with cover art, playtime, last played and achievements. A fallback scanner also finds games installed outside any launcher.

  • Achievements, friends list and OS notifications on new unlocks
  • Backlog tags, favourites, hidden games and playtime stats
  • Per-game and per-drive disk usage view
  • Runs entirely locally with a sandboxed renderer and a minimal IPC surface

~/disk-usage-manager in development

Disk Usage Manager

See where your disk went, then reclaim it safely.

A fast, visual alternative to WinDirStat and TreeSize. A parallel scanner walks the drive and streams progress while an animated treemap lets you zoom into folders. Anything you delete goes to the recycle bin, never a hard delete.

  • Squarified treemap and sunburst views over the same data
  • Parallel scanning with jwalk and rayon
  • SQLite snapshot cache: reopen scans, diff growth over time
  • Confirm-then-trash deletion flow

~/new-win-dock in development

New Win Dock

A macOS-style dock to replace the Windows 11 taskbar.

The stock taskbar can't be themed or tuned much. This native Rust dock sits where the taskbar normally is, with pinned and running apps, a Start button that hands off to the real Windows search, a system tray, and a clock and quick-settings cluster.

  • Runtime toggle to hide or show the real taskbar
  • Auto-hides when a fullscreen app is in the foreground
  • Webview-based settings panel, launches at startup
